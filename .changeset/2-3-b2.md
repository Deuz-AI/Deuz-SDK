Native budget reservations now cover what the provider can actually bill. A call used to reserve `maxOutputTokens ?? 4096` output tokens while the request asked for `maxOutputTokens` or the model's full `maxOutput`, and Anthropic raises even an explicit cap to the thinking budget plus 1 024. Parallel calls could therefore spend far past a USD budget (a $0.50 budget admitted $10). The reservation now prices the output cap the wire sends, reasoning included, and prices a prompt the call caches at the cache-write rate on Anthropic, the only wire that writes a cache; elsewhere it stays input, long-context tiers included. A row that documents no output limit (an unknown model, and rows such as `gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-5.6` and `gpt-5-pro`) sends no cap on OpenAI Chat Completions, OpenAI Responses and Gemini native; under a token or USD bound, in the context's budget or a persistent scope, such a call now sends the row's 4 096-token placeholder as `maxOutputTokens`, reserves exactly that and reports a `clamped-setting` warning on `maxOutputTokens`, so its reservation stays a bound. A call without a bound still leaves the cap off. Because reservations are larger, a call without `maxOutputTokens` on a large-output model can now be refused with `budget_exceeded` under a small USD budget; set `maxOutputTokens` to fit the budget. Admission remains an estimate, not a provider-enforced cap.

A model with no price no longer breaks USD budgets. A model is unpriced when no `deps.priceProvider` is configured or the provider returns `undefined` for it. **Behaviour change:** under a USD budget, native runs (`runAgent`, `generateText`/`streamChat` with an execution context, swarms, `evolve` and `BudgetStore` admission) used to refuse such a call with `missing_reservation` before any model call. They now run it: its tokens are still reserved, bounded and settled, its cost is left out of the USD totals (`totals().unpriced` counts these calls, which stay in `unknownUsd`), and the run reports the new `unpriced-model` warning once per model. **`CallWarning['type']` is widened with `'unpriced-model'`.** Set `budget.unknownPrice: 'reject'` to keep the old fail-closed behaviour: the call is then refused before it is sent, with `BudgetLedgerError` code `unpriced_model` (`BudgetLedgerErrorCode` is widened with `'unpriced_model'`). The policy lives on `BudgetLimits` (`unknownPrice?: 'warn' | 'reject'`, type `UnknownPricePolicy`), so it applies to every budget: contexts and their children, swarm, task and reducer budgets, `evolve`, and persistent scope limits; `'reject'` always wins when budgets intersect. A price lookup that throws still fails a USD bound closed. In the agentic loop without an execution context, one unpriced call used to switch `budget.usd` and `costExceeds` off for the rest of the run; unpriced calls now add nothing to the cost and the priced calls keep counting.

Unpriced holds close. In a `BudgetStore`, an unpriced call settles its token hold to the actual usage and holds no USD; a call reserved with a caller `executionEstimate.usd` for a model that turns out to have no price settles that estimate as its charge instead of keeping the hold forever.

Settlements survive a failing store. The native request forgot an attempt's request ID before settling it, so a failed settlement left the hold at its estimate with no way to find it. The ledger now tries a failing settlement or release three times, the native request keeps the request ID until the ledger accepts the write and gives a failed settlement one more round of three, completing the call normally when that round closes the hold, and the final `admission_failed` error carries `requestId` and, for a settlement, `settlement`, which `ledger.settle(error.settlement)` replays once the store is back. A settlement or release that the store refuses with `reservation_conflict` found its hold already closed, for example by a `sweep` that got there first: it is neither retried nor reported, the local entry closes, and the store keeps the sweep's amounts.

Each warning is reported once. A warning a call records through a logger-backed sink (`generateObject`, `streamObject`, the agentic loops, native finalization) was logged twice, and one recorded after the sink's 50-entry list cap was not logged at all; both now log one line. A streaming loop no longer streams a warning its step's own stream already carried, and the `unpriced-model` warning reaches the stream ahead of the model's output, also when the run's only model call meets the model.

Additive types: `BudgetLimits.unknownPrice`, `UnknownPricePolicy`, `BudgetAmounts` (the `{ tokens, usd }` shape that `BudgetTotals` and `BudgetAggregate` used to type as `Required<BudgetLimits>`), `BudgetReservationInput.unpriced`, `BudgetReservation.unpriced`, `BudgetTotals.unpriced`, `BudgetAggregate.unpricedUsd`, and `BudgetLedgerError.requestId` and `.settlement`. Snapshots gain only optional fields; a 2.2 reader that restores one keeps failing a USD bound closed on unpriced entries.
